Set it once. Stop thinking about it.

Most schedule apps break the first time your school does something weird. Quasar was built around the weird parts.

Now and next, with a countdown

Open the app and see the period you're in, how much of it is left, and what follows. No tapping through a grid to figure out your own day.

Any rotation, really

Numbered cycles, letter days, periods that move position day to day, anchors and resets after a closure. Built from a real 10‑day rotating schedule, not a demo.

Offline by default

Every edit saves to your device first and uploads when you're back. Basement classroom, airplane mode, dead campus Wi‑Fi — it still works.

The rotation is the hard part.
So we started there.

  • Structured editors, never JSON. Periods, cycle days, lunch waves and exceptions are forms — with a live preview of real dates before you save.
  • Shared, then locked. Your school's schedule is built once by students, and locks at ten members so nobody can quietly break it.
  • Your overrides survive. Shift a day, close a date, add a one‑off period. School corrections never silently overwrite your personal changes.
